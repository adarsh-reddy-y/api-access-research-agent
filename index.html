100 apps: who lets an agent in, and how?

Auth, access, API surface, MCP and buildability for the 100 apps on the Composio take-home list. Research run 23 Nov 2026 (docs and pricing change; each row has its evidence link). 2-minute read: patterns, then the agent, then proof, then where it was wrong.

1. Patterns up top

85 of 100 take an API key/token71 support OAuth2. 58 offer both, 27 are key/token only, 13 are OAuth2 only. Keys are the easy door; OAuth is for multi-user apps.
58 free self-serveSign up, get a key, call the API, no human. Another 22 need a paid or trial account (S2).
20 gated12 need app review or approval (G1), 8 need a sales or partner contract (G2). Most common blocker overall: a paid plan (S2), then app review.
90 of 100 have an official MCPMost vendors now ship one. MCP is no longer the differentiator; access to the API is.

3. The agent

1 List100 app names and hints pulled from the assignment page (lazy tables, needed a browser scroll).
2 Search3 searches per app (docs/auth, MCP, access/pricing): 300 results files. search.py
3 FetchPull the doc pages and keep auth/access signal snippets. fetch_evidence.py
4 Rules baselineKeyword rules, no LLM. This is the weak first pass. pass1_rules.py
5 LLM reviewAn AI model reads each app's evidence and writes a 13-field record with a confidence and a check flag. 53 flags.
6 Verify loopTargeted searches and direct doc fetches on flagged apps; corrections logged old to new. apply_corrections.py
7 AuditSeeded random sample of 25, fresh fetch, score vs truth. verify.py

Where a human was needed. (a) Getting the app list out of lazy-rendered Notion tables (I had to scroll a browser). (b) Disambiguating vendors when search returned a look-alike (Otter, Sherlock, Mermaid, Paygent, YouTube Transcript). (c) Judging gated vs paid (S2 vs G1) when docs are vague. (d) Sign-off on the 3 lowest-confidence rows. Steps 5 to 7 were done by an AI assistant working in the repo workspace, not by an unattended script: the scripts do search, fetch, baseline and scoring; the reading and judgement is the model. A human (Adarsh) has not yet spot-checked rows; the queue for that check is section 6.

7. Where the agent was wrong or an app defeated it

  • Wrong vendor from search: Otter (hit a food-delivery product), Mermaid (datamermaid.org), Sherlock (AI products), YouTube Transcript (look-alikes). Caught at review, not by the script.
  • Paygent Connect: unresolved. The list hint says "NMI-powered". I found Paygent products that are AI-agent wallets, not NMI gateways. The row is marked not verified.
  • Copper MCP was reported as a live MCP in pass 2; the vendor page says "coming soon".
  • WhatsApp Business was "Blocked until outreach" in pass 2; Meta's docs show a self-serve test token, so it is now "Ready with caveat" (production needs business verification).
  • Tier flips: Squarespace (G1 to S2: paid plan, not review), Neo4j Aura (S1 to S2: card on file), QuickBooks (S1 to G1 for production: assessment), MrScraper (S2 to S1), YouTube Transcript (S2 to S1), Clay (S2 to S1).
  • Pages the fetcher could not read: Lark docs (JS-rendered), Salesforce Commerce Cloud docs (403), Binance docs (JS). These rely on search excerpts or other pages.
  • Third-party only: DealCloud and Pylon being sales-gated rests on G2 and vendor-adjacent pages, not an official statement. Pylon says "trial via sales" (third party).
  • Noise: a repeated odd snippet on several fetched vendor pages claimed "free tier, no form"; I treated it as unreliable and ignored it.
  • Not done: No live API calls were made to apps to prove access. Everything is from docs, which can be stale or wrong.
